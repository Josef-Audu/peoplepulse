# **01\. EXECUTIVE DEFINITION**

## **1.1 What is PeoplePulse?**

PeoplePulse is a human evidence platform that helps people understand what other people think, need, want, experience, and why, before making decisions that involve them.

PeoplePulse turns uncertainty about people into structured human evidence.

The core workflow is:

**Decision → Assumptions → Audience → Pulse → Responses → Why → Evidence → Decision**

The Pulse is the mechanism.

**The evidence is the product.**

---

## **1.2 The problem**

People constantly make decisions based on assumptions about other people.

An entrepreneur assumes customers will buy.

A creator assumes an audience wants certain content.

A product designer assumes users understand a feature.

A school assumes students need something.

A researcher wants to understand a behavior.

An organization wants to know what its community thinks.

The underlying problem is the same:

> **We often make decisions about people without enough evidence from the people themselves.**

Existing tools help users create forms, polls, surveys, or recruit research participants.

PeoplePulse is designed around the larger question:

> **What are you trying to understand or decide, and what evidence do you need before acting?**

---

# **02\. PRODUCT THESIS**

PeoplePulse operates on several fundamental beliefs.

### **2.1 The question is not the product.**

A question is merely a mechanism for gathering information.

The real product relationship is:

**Decision → Question → Human response → Evidence → Understanding**

---

### **2.2 More responses do not automatically mean better evidence.**

A large sample can still be biased, self-selected, poorly designed, or irrelevant to the question.

PeoplePulse therefore presents response data together with appropriate context.

---

### **2.3 Quantitative data tells us what.**

Qualitative evidence can help us understand why.

Therefore PeoplePulse connects:

**What? → Why?**

through the Ask Why experience.

---

### **2.4 Evidence has boundaries.**

PeoplePulse must never turn a sample into an unsupported claim about an entire population.

For example:

Incorrect:

> 64% of Nigerians want this.

Correct:

> 64% of PeoplePulse respondents selected this option.

Where appropriate, the interface should expose:

* participant count  
* audience definition  
* collection period  
* recruitment method  
* sampling limitations  
* evidence context

---

### **2.5 AI should amplify evidence, never manufacture it.**

AI may eventually help researchers:

* identify themes  
* summarize qualitative responses  
* improve research questions  
* compare studies  
* detect patterns

But AI must not create evidence that was never collected.

# **05\. USERS**

## **5.1 Primary Persona: The Decision Maker**

The Decision Maker is anyone who needs to make a decision involving other people but lacks sufficient evidence.

This is a behavioral persona rather than a professional category.

Potential users include:

* entrepreneurs  
* creators  
* product teams  
* researchers  
* students  
* educators  
* organizations  
* community leaders  
* marketers  
* institutions

### **Core job**

> “Help me understand the people affected by this decision before I act.”

### **Needs**

* clarity  
* relevant participants  
* fast research  
* understandable results  
* evidence context  
* qualitative explanation  
* actionable findings

### **Frustrations**

* guessing  
* unreliable anecdotes  
* scattered feedback  
* poor survey responses

* difficult analysis

* confusing questions

# **07\. MVP PRODUCT DEFINITION**

## **7.1 MVP objective**

The MVP must answer one fundamental question:

> **Can PeoplePulse help someone move from uncertainty about people to useful evidence that informs a real decision?**

---

## **7.2 MVP core loop**

### **Step 1: Decision**

User states what they are trying to decide.

### **Step 2: Assumptions**

User identifies what they currently believe.

### **Step 3: Audience**

User defines who needs to provide evidence.

### **Step 4: Pulse**

User creates a short research instrument.

### **Step 5: Responses**

Participants provide answers.

### **Step 6: Ask Why**

Participants provide qualitative explanations where useful.

### **Step 7: Evidence**

PeoplePulse presents structured findings with context.

### **Step 8: Decision Brief**

PeoplePulse packages the research into a concise decision-oriented artifact.

# **8.12 Evidence Context**

Every meaningful finding should be presented with enough context to avoid misleading interpretation.

Example:

> **64%**

> would consider using the service

> **1,284 participants**

> University students · 18–25

> **Early signal**

> Self-selected sample

The evidence context should communicate limitations without overwhelming the user.

---

# **8.13 Ask Why**

Ask Why is a signature PeoplePulse feature.

When a quantitative result raises an important question, PeoplePulse should provide a path toward qualitative explanation.

Example:

> 64% selected Yes.

> **Why?**

Possible MVP implementation:

* optional open-text follow-up within the Pulse  
* targeted follow-up question  
* separate short follow-up Pulse

The MVP does not require a real-time messaging system.

---

# **8.14 Findings**

Findings organize important observations from research.

A finding may contain:

* statement  
* supporting response data  
* qualitative explanations  
* audience context  
* evidence context  
* limitations

**Observed pattern**

from:

**Interpretation**

and:

**Prediction**

> **A modern editorial research document.**

### **Structure**

**PEOPLEPULSE**

**DECISION BRIEF**

Project name

---

### **The Question**

What were we trying to understand?

### **The Audience**

Unexpected findings.

PeoplePulse should follow data minimization.

* authentication  
* research eligibility  
* research participation  
* quality control  
* product operation

Participants should understand:

* why the research is being conducted  
* what information is collected  
* how responses are used  
* whether responses are anonymous  
* whether responses may be aggregated  
* how data can be deleted where applicable

Privacy Policy and Terms of Service are required before public launch.

# **15\. RESEARCH INTEGRITY**

PeoplePulse must distinguish between:

### **Measurement**

> 1,284 people answered.

### **Pattern**

> 64% selected A.

### **Interpretation**

> A appears more common among this respondent group.

### **Prediction**

> A will succeed in the market.

Each step introduces additional uncertainty.

The interface must avoid silently converting one into another.

# **28\. MVP SUCCESS METRICS**

Raw user count should not be the first measure of success.

The MVP should measure whether the research loop works.

## **Primary metrics**

### **Project completion rate**

Percentage of users who move from:

**Project creation → completed research**

---

### **Pulse completion rate**

Percentage of participants who start and finish a Pulse.

---

### **Evidence usefulness**

After completing research, determine whether users report that the evidence:

* changed their decision  
* strengthened their decision  
* challenged an assumption  
* revealed something unexpected  
* identified a new question

---

### **Repeat research**

Do users create another Project after completing one?

This is a major signal of product value.

---

### **Qualitative participation**

How often do participants provide useful explanations when asked why?

---

### **Evidence comprehension**

Can users correctly understand the limitations of their sample?

# **34\. THE PEOPLEPULSE TEST**

Before adding a feature, ask:

### **Does it help someone understand people?**

If no, question it.

### **Does it improve the quality of evidence?**

If no, question it.

### **Does it help someone make a decision?**

If no, question it.

### **Does it increase unnecessary complexity?**

If yes, question it.

### **Does it collect personal data we don't need?**

If yes, remove it.

### **Does it create false certainty?**

If yes, redesign it.

---

# **35\. FINAL DEFINITION**

PeoplePulse is not fundamentally a place to ask questions.

It is a system for moving from:

> **“I think people want this.”**

to:

> **“Here is what the people we studied told us.”**

and eventually:

> **“Here is what we understand, what remains uncertain, and what we should investigate next.”**

The Pulse collects the signal.

The system structures it.

The evidence reveals the pattern.

The Decision Brief turns understanding into something usable.

The decision comes last.

**People are the signal.**  
**Evidence is the product.**  
**Better decisions are the outcome.**
